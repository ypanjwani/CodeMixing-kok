  • Real: LinCE Hindi-English, with gold labels on every word, and the authors' English-Kokborok corpus (6,986 unique sentences).
  • Silver labels: the English-Kokborok words were tagged automatically, using the English-Kokborok-Bengali Dictionary (Binoy Debbarma) plus context rules. A native speaker has not checked them.
  • Synthetic: FLORES-200 and MCS-350 single-language sentences were mixed by inserting 1–3-word spans of the other language. The Kokborok-Bengali sentences replace the English spans in real Kokborok text with romanized Bengali.
  • Romanized: Hindi and Bengali are written in Latin script, as on social media.
  • No leakage: each source sentence was assigned to train, validation or test before mixing.

03Model and training

The setup follows the paper's Table 5: trainable 300-d embeddings, Conv1D (128 filters, kernel 3, ReLU), max-pooling, BiLSTM (128), dropout 0.5 and a softmax over 5 languages for each word. Training uses Adam (lr 0.001, β₁ 0.9, β₂ 0.999), batch 64, up to 50 epochs, with early stopping on validation loss.

Deviations from the paper

  1. Max-pooling uses stride 1. Pooling over the whole sentence would leave one vector per sentence, which makes per-word labels impossible.
  2. PyTorch instead of Keras. The architecture is the same.
  3. Hardware: one RTX 3050 laptop GPU. Cooling pauses are excluded from the training times.
  4. SGD uses momentum 0.9. All optimizers share the learning rate 0.001.

  • The BiLSTM alone scored 93.9%, higher than the CNN-BiLSTM. Convolution and pooling blend each word with its neighbours, which blurs the signal at language-switch points.
  • The CNN alone sees only a 3-word window and is the weakest model (83.2%).
  • The Transformer trained from scratch (90.0%) has no pretraining and only about 38k training sentences.

Preliminary, not a result: an interrupted mBERT run reached 96.9% validation accuracy after one epoch, above the proposed model. The paper's claim that the proposed model beats BERT, mBERT and XLM-R should not be kept until these runs are completed.

  • Most frequent error: Spanish read as English (659 tokens).
  • Shared words such as a, no, me, come, na, ki exist in more than one language.
  • Romanized Hindi and Bengali share spellings (ke, ki, ar, kare), which causes most Bengali↔Hindi errors.
  • Unknown words: 9.0% of test words never appear in training. A character-level layer would help.
  • Kokborok: part of the error there is noise in the automatic labels.

09Limitations

  1. 75% of the data is synthetic and does not follow real code-switching grammar.
  2. The English-Kokborok labels are automatic and need a native-speaker check.
  3. No real Kokborok-Bengali code-mixed text was available.
  4. Stride-1 max-pooling is our reading of the paper's architecture.
  5. The mBERT and XLM-R baselines were not completed because of time and hardware limits.

Before submission, the paper's dataset section must describe the synthetic data and the silver labels, and the claims marked "Does not hold" or "Not verified" above should be revised.
